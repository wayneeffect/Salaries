**Entry-level Software Architect roles (often requiring prior software engineering experience) typically start in the $100,000–$140,000 range nationally in the US; mid-level around $135,000–$170,000; senior levels commonly $150,000–$200,000+ base, with total compensation frequently reaching $180,000–$300,000+ (and higher at large tech firms) when including bonuses and equity.**

These are approximate 2026 US national figures drawn from Robert Half, PayScale, Glassdoor, Indeed, and other sources. Software Architect roles focus on high-level software system design, technical standards, scalability, integration, and guiding development teams. Pure zero-experience entry positions are uncommon—many expect several years of software engineering or related architecture background. Pay varies by specialization (e.g., enterprise, cloud, microservices), company size/industry, location, and total package.

### Factors That Influence Pay
- **Location** — High-cost tech hubs (Bay Area, Seattle, NYC) often add 20–40%+ premiums; remote roles may align closer to national medians.
- **Skills & domain** — Cloud-native design, microservices, distributed systems, security, scalability, specific stacks (e.g., enterprise Java/.NET, modern web/cloud), or domain expertise (fintech, healthcare) increase value.
- **Company type** — Large tech, finance, or high-growth companies typically offer stronger total compensation (especially equity/RSUs) than smaller firms or non-tech industries.
- **Scope** — Application-level vs. enterprise/system-wide architecture; hands-on coding vs. pure design/strategy.
