**Entry-level Hardware Architect roles (often expecting related engineering experience) typically start in the $100,000–$140,000 range nationally in the US; mid-level around $120,000–$180,000; senior levels commonly $150,000–$250,000+ base, with total compensation frequently reaching $200,000–$350,000+ (and higher at major semiconductor/tech firms) when including bonuses and equity.**

These are approximate 2026 US national figures synthesized from Indeed, Glassdoor, Salary.com, ZipRecruiter, Levels.fyi (for related Hardware Engineer roles), and other sources. “Hardware Architect” (sometimes titled Computer Hardware Architect, Hardware Systems Architect, or similar) involves high-level design of computer hardware systems, components, platforms, or systems architecture—often in semiconductors, servers, embedded systems, or computing platforms. Pure zero-experience entry roles are uncommon; many positions expect prior hardware engineering, systems design, or related architecture experience. Pay varies widely by specialization (e.g., GPU/ASIC, server platforms, networking hardware), company (especially NVIDIA, Intel, Apple, AMD, or big tech), location, and total package.

### Factors That Influence Pay
- **Location** — Silicon Valley, Seattle, Austin, and other tech hubs often add significant premiums; remote or lower-cost areas pay less.
- **Specialization & skills** — ASIC/FPGA design, system-on-chip, server/platform architecture, high-performance computing, power/thermal design, or mixed hardware-software systems command higher pay.
- **Company type** — Semiconductor leaders (NVIDIA, Intel, AMD, Arm, Broadcom), big tech, or automotive/compute hardware firms typically offer stronger total compensation (especially equity) than smaller or non-tech employers.
- **Background alignment** — Hands-on hardware design, systems integration, and architecture experience are highly valued.
