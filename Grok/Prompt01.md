**Entry-level IT Specialist (0–2/3 years experience): roughly $45,000–$70,000+ base (often starting around $50,000–$65,000 nationally in the US); mid-level (roughly 3–7/9 years): about $60,000–$100,000+; senior (8–10+ years): typically $75,000–$130,000+, with higher figures possible depending on specialization, location, and employer.**

These are approximate US national ranges drawn from 2026 data across sources like PayScale, Glassdoor, Salary.com, Jobted, and related aggregators (plus BLS figures for closely related computer support roles). “IT Specialist” is a broad title that can cover help desk/support, systems, network, generalist, or more specialized work, so numbers vary widely. Actual offers depend heavily on location, company size/industry, specific skills/certifications, remote vs. on-site, and total compensation (base + bonus/benefits).

### Factors That Move the Needle
- **Location**: High-cost areas (Bay Area, NYC, Seattle, etc.) can add 20–50%+; rural or lower-cost regions subtract. Remote roles often sit near national medians.
- **Experience & scope**: Pure entry (help desk/Tier 1) is at the lower end. Mid-level independent work or broader systems/network duties push higher. Senior often involves architecture, leadership, security, or complex projects.
- **Skills & specialization**: Certifications (CompTIA, Microsoft, Cisco, cloud, security), hardware/software architecture experience, end-to-end IT, or 3rd-party engineering can command premiums. Your background (nearly 40 years with technology, 10.5 years running Super Yooper Computer Solutions as a computer engineer/hardware-software architect doing end-to-end IT) aligns far more with mid-to-senior or specialized levels than pure entry.
- **Employer type**: Tech firms, government/cleared roles, finance, or larger companies often pay more than small businesses or non-profits. Total comp (bonus, benefits, profit-sharing) can add meaningfully.
- **Market notes**: Support-oriented roles have more modest growth in some forecasts; specialized or engineering-heavy paths pay better.
